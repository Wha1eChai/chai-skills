# Surface and Style Guidance

Read before choosing/changing visual direction, resolving conflicting visual baselines, or deciding cross-surface consistency. A substantial layout change alone does not trigger this reference when existing expression remains clear and appropriate. Missing DESIGN.md alone is also not a trigger.

**Working output:** dominant surface purpose, preserved identity, relevant expression axes, and allowed variation with reasons. For advice, deliver a concise recommendation; for a design proposal, provide a concrete direction or useful specimen. For implementation, apply the direction rather than stopping at a style description. Persist in existing project guidance only when durable documentation is requested; use Project Design for a new/evolving project baseline.

This reference owns expression direction, not task structure or code-level token migration. A direction is an expression choice, not a quality rank. These options are starting points, not a closed style catalog.

## Choose purpose first

| Purpose | User success | Positive design outcome | Available expressive means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Operate | Complete a task accurately and efficiently | Important states stand out, actions are discoverable, continuous work feels fluent | Readable density, clear grouping, recognizable controls, distinctive color/type, responsive feedback |
| Read | Find and understand information | Answers are easy to locate, explanations invite continued reading, examples are clear | Wayfinding, typographic character, reading rhythm, diagrams, imagery, color-led orientation |
| Decide | Understand alternatives and make an informed choice | Value is quickly understood, evidence earns attention, consequences and next steps are clear | Comparable attributes, persuasive composition, strong brand expression, useful demonstrations |
| Experience | Inspect or experience the work | The artifact invites attention and detail rewards exploration | Media scale, framing, spatial composition, expressive transitions, accessible viewing controls |

Use these purposes to identify what deserves attention; tone and expressive intensity come from the audience, content, brand, and brief. An editor can be vivid, and a gallery can be quiet. Combine purposes at surface or region level as needed.

## Determine what is already decided

Inspect existing brand assets, design guidance, tokens, components, and representative pages. Identify:
- Stable identity: type roles, palette relationships, icon language, shape, motion character.
- Stable behavior: navigation, controls, state vocabulary, conventions users already know.
- Accidental drift: unexplained overrides, inconsistent spacing, duplicate components.

For refinement, develop the established identity; for a new page, extend its language. Resolve conflicting baselines using the entry's authority guidance, then make the chosen direction tangible through composition, type, color, and detail.

## Describe a direction with useful axes

For meaningful new design work, describe only the relevant axes in a short working note:
- **Tone:** matter-of-fact, welcoming, editorial, playful, expressive, or another specific intention.
- **Density:** reading, balanced, or compact; which regions differ and why.
- **Composition:** comparison, list-detail, single task, narrative, media-led, or another task-derived structure.
- **Typography:** roles, language coverage, personality, readability.
- **Color and depth:** brand/action/status roles; surface and overlay relationships.
- **Motion:** feedback, continuity, pacing, or expressive presence; match timing to use frequency and interaction.

Avoid arbitrary numerical taste scores. A setting such as "variance 8" does not explain what changes or why it is appropriate.

## Optional expression families

### Clear utility

For tools and self-service, make information easy to scan and actions easy to recognize through grouping, alignment, readable density, and responsive feedback. Express personality through color, typography, imagery, language, and precise details as the brief calls for.

### Editorial reading

For knowledge and content, make reading inviting through type character, comfortable measure, section rhythm, and expressive or explanatory imagery. Choose fonts for the language, content, and intended voice; let headings and navigation provide clear orientation.

### Confident brand

For introductions, campaigns, and propositions, develop a recognizable visual idea through composition, typography, imagery, or color. Make the value tangible, support claims with real evidence, and give the next action a clear place in the composition.

### Artifact-led creation and display

Good for media, portfolios, galleries, and creative tools. Respect the artifact's scale and aspect ratio. Separate editing controls, status, and output. Distinguish inspirational examples from actual generated or uploaded work.

These families can combine and are not mandatory styles. Other directions are valid when justified by the brief.

## When no style is specified

For existing work, develop its recognizable strengths toward the requested improvement. For genuinely new work, use content and audience to choose an appealing direction, then coordinate readable type, clear priorities, color, imagery, space, and detail. Treat visual appeal as part of the goal from the start.

Make a reasonable low-risk recommendation and proceed. When a material choice remains unresolved, a small visual comparison can clarify composition, density, color character, typography, or imagery. Hold relevant content and constraints constant so the difference is understandable.

## Interpret visual feedback at the right level

Translate feedback such as "more contrast", "too gray", "more premium", or "more eye-catching" into the intended experience. Contrast may concern text readability, attention hierarchy, or the palette's overall character. Read the screenshot and conversation together, identify the likely target, and recommend concrete changes to the relevant relationships. Ask a focused question only when materially different interpretations remain.

When preferences become clearer, describe the newly selected direction accurately. Keep taste choices distinct from demonstrable defects. For durable project guidance, record the intended character and important visual roles alongside the implementation source.

## Consistency across surfaces

Share the meanings and relationships that help recognition: terminology, interaction consequences, state semantics, icon conventions, and selected brand cues. Allow different density, composition, reading width, and expression where tasks differ.

A shared semantic token name may have different values in different themes. Distinct frameworks do not have to share component code. Do not force one technical or visual architecture merely because sites share ownership.

## Reference use

Borrow a specific relationship: hierarchy, density, typography, rhythm, or interaction. Carry it into the project's content and established system rather than copying the source's identity. Respect asset rights and keep project facts authoritative.

For concrete alternatives, read the matching case in [Positive design examples](positive-examples.md): Case 1 for composition, Case 2 for two voices within one brand, or Case 3 for spatial rhythm. Use a specimen when it helps decide; otherwise apply the selected direction directly. Implementation evidence follows Verification.
